# Expansion fit: which other small-business categories share the furniture pricing problem
Generated: 2026-10-25 · Agent: research-expansion · Status: partial

Scope: categories beyond independent furniture where sales are negotiated, items are priced one by one, stock ages, staff work on commission, and the owner is not always on the floor. Census store counts are out of scope (separate agent). 30-minute time box; most vendor prices are third-party listings or estimates because vendors in these verticals quote privately. Fit scores and the "top 4" are this agent's judgment, not source claims.

## Summary
- Used-car dealers already pay for exactly this: CarGurus' average U.S. paying dealer spent $7,337 per quarter (about $2,450/month) in Q4 2024 and $7,938 (about $2,650/month) in Q4 2025, and aged-car holding cost is put at $30-85 per car per day [F-expansion-01, F-expansion-03, F-expansion-04].
- Used cars are proof of willingness to pay, not a first target: vAuto, CarGurus and DMS desking tools already own the negotiation-and-approval workflow [F-expansion-05, F-expansion-06].
- Independent jewelers fit best: inventory turns about 0.7-1.2 times a year, independents bargain more than chains, and pay is often 15-16% of the gross profit each salesperson makes, yet the main POS (The Edge, about $3,500-4,600 one-time) has no tool for checking an offer against a minimum [F-expansion-08, F-expansion-09, F-expansion-10, F-expansion-11].
- In boat, RV and powersports dealers, aging is a cash cost: floor-plan rates were 8.07-10.57% in March 2024, with the lender's margin rising as units age. Northpoint said more than 20% of 2024 inventory was over 12 months old. Lightspeed DMS is estimated at $1,000-3,000+ per store per month [F-expansion-14, F-expansion-15, F-expansion-17].
- Pawn and consignment stores price every item by hand and haggle constantly, but they pay little for software ($60-333/month for pawn, $159-238/month for Ricochet), so the per-store price would have to be lower [F-expansion-18, F-expansion-19].
- Analyst sizing of pricing software varies with the definition: $1.4-2.0B for "price optimization" in 2025, $2.1-3.05B for "dynamic pricing software" in 2024, and $12.4B for "retail pricing software" in 2025, at 9-20% CAGR. These are vendor-report estimates, and there is no reliable SMB adoption figure [F-expansion-22, F-expansion-23, F-expansion-24].

## Findings

### F-expansion-01 — CarGurus' average U.S. paying dealer spends about $2,450-2,650 a month
- **Value:** U.S. QARSD (quarterly average revenue per subscribing dealer) was $7,337 in Q4 2024 and $7,938 in Q4 2025, or about $2,446 and $2,646 a month (monthly figures are this agent's arithmetic). Q4 2023 was $6,532.
- **Source:** [CarGurus, Inc. FY2025 Annual Report (Form ARS)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001494259/000119312526170497/2025_form_ars.pdf), SEC filing, 2026
- **Confidence:** high (audited company metric). It is marketplace and listing spend, which includes CarGurus' IMV price ratings, not a pricing tool alone.
- **Use:** pitch

### F-expansion-08 — Jewelry stores turn inventory only about 0.7-1.2 times a year
- **Value:** The average U.S. jewelry store turns inventory 0.7-1.2 times a year, so a piece sits about 10-17 months on average (this agent's arithmetic). The average GMROI across one consultant's surveyed stores was 70 (called weak). One owner keeps aged stock at or below 20%, and some groups count anything over 365 days as aged.
- **Source:** [INSTORE: Jewelry retailers split on inventory turn, clearance showcases](https://instoremag.com/jewelry-retailers-split-on-inventory-turn-clearance-showcases), INSTORE, date n/a. [David Brown column (Edge Retail Academy) on GMROI](https://lsc-pagepro.mydigitalpublication.com/article/David+Brown/1813835/225881/article.html), trade press, date n/a

- **Use:** pitch

### F-expansion-09 — Independent jewelers bargain more than chains, especially on pieces that have aged
- **Value:** Consumer Reports' 2009 poll: 79% of jewelry hagglers got a lower price, the highest of any category. A jeweler told Time (2012) that chains have firm corporate policies while locally owned stores can decide quickly, and that a piece that has sat in the showroom is easier to discount. A jeweler's blog calls 10-20% off realistic and 5-10% for cash.
- **Source:** [Consumer Reports press release, May 2009](https://www.consumerreports.org/media-room/press-releases/2009/05/consumer-reports-money-special-publication-haggling-for-everything-is-new-rule-in-a-down-economy), 2009. ### F-expansion-13 — Appliance minimum prices are set by manufacturers (UMRP), which leaves little room for a store-set minimum

The BrandSource buying group counts about 4,500 independent dealers with $16B+ in sales (undated), and Nationwide Marketing Group about 5,000 members (2022).

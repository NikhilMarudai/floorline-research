## Summary
- The buyer universe is small and skewed tiny: 21,964 US furniture-store establishments in 2023, 11,483 of them with under 5 employees; California has 2,203, and 2,068 (94%) have fewer than 20 employees [F-gtm-01, F-gtm-02].
- Five organizations already aggregate most independents and sell vendor access: HFA (1,500+ members, 13,000+ storefronts, California-headquartered), Nationwide (5,500+ members, furniture division 2,600+), BrandSource (4,500+ stores), Furniture First (223 retailers / 520 stores, the closest fit to a $2M+ independent), MEGA Group (1,700 members) [F-gtm-05 to F-gtm-10].
- Independents already pay $89–$399 per month per location for single-purpose SaaS (Lightspeed $89–$289 per register, Square for Retail Plus $89/location, Clientbook ~$295, Podium $399 for 2 locations, Wondersign $168 per kiosk); per-location-per-month is the dominant model, and no percent-of-savings retail SaaS was found [F-gtm-21 to F-gtm-25].
- The strongest value anchors: a 1% price improvement lifts operating profit ~8% for a typical S&P 1500 firm (McKinsey, secondary citation); tightening who may discount recovered 3 margin points at a 450-staff services firm (consultant case); a 700-store retailer banked $123k margin in 30 days from reducing price overrides (vendor case); analysts and vendors converge on 1–2 margin points from pricing discipline [F-gtm-26 to F-gtm-30].
- Bain's 1,700-company survey gives the problem statement in one line: misaligned incentives "push deals down to the minimum allowed price," and only 26% of companies use pricing software though users report 2.5x stronger outcomes [F-gtm-27].
- Adoption risk is real and documented: owners fear system conversion and losing customer history; merchants switch tools for missing features (34%) and poor support (27%) before price (26%); salespeople resist price controls unless commission is tied to margin [F-gtm-32 to F-gtm-34].

### (c) Draft "path to first 100" (adapt; each claim carries its finding ID)

Start where the buyer already is, not where software is sold. California alone has 2,203 furniture stores and 94% of them have fewer than 20 employees [F-gtm-02], so the first ten customers come from walk-ins within driving distance, with Mona as the reference store. Customers 40–100 come through aggregators that vet vendors once and then recommend them to hundreds of owners: Furniture First's 223 retailers are exactly the $2M+ full-line independent this product serves and its Performance Groups put 8–15 owners in a room three times a year [F-gtm-07]; Nationwide's furniture division (2,600+ members) and BrandSource (4,500+ stores) follow once there are case numbers [F-gtm-08, F-gtm-09]; and the 2,000+ manufacturer reps who already call on every store can carry a referral [F-gtm-14]. The message is the same at every step: owners pay $89–$399 a month per store for single-purpose tools today [F-gtm-21 to F-gtm-23]; pricing discipline is worth 1–3 margin points in the only cases on record [F-gtm-28 to F-gtm-30], which is $30,000 per point for a $3M store [F-gtm-31]; and the product does not replace the POS, which is the thing owners fear [F-gtm-33].

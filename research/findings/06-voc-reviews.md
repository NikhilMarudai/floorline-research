# Voice of customer — review sites (customers and furniture sales staff)
Generated: 2026-10-24 · Agent: research-voc-reviews · Status: partial (Yelp, Indeed, Glassdoor, ConsumerAffairs blocked direct fetch; web-search budget exhausted mid-task)

## Summary
- Customers repeatedly report the same item priced differently between visits, between salespeople, and between store and website; the gap is often hundreds of dollars and the store usually refuses to honor the earlier number [Q-reviews-01, 02, 03, 04, 07, 08, 18].
- "Had to get the manager" is a routine step in final pricing; customers experience it as either a win ("went to his boss got me a better price") or an insult, and salespeople are described as having "no discretion to make exceptions" [Q-reviews-05, 06, 09, 10].
- Delivery fees are the most-contested line item: promised free then charged, varying by day, un-waivable "new policy", or waived only after escalation [Q-reviews-06, 08, 14, 15, 16, 19, 20].
- On the staff side, commission is tied to delivered revenue and is eroded by manager discounts, returns and damaged-goods markdowns the salesperson did not choose [Q-reviews-22, 23, 24, 27].
- Draw-against-commission and commission-on-margin plans make every discount a direct pay cut for the salesperson, which is the structural source of the discount tug-of-war the product targets [Q-reviews-28, 33, 36, 37].
- Pay rules and pricing change often and unpredictably from the floor's point of view ("change rules daily", "pay structure changes almost monthly", "commission pay changes yearly") [Q-reviews-25, 31, 35].

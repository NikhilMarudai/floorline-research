# Owner interview

The owner-operator of a family-run furniture store near Sacramento, California, interviewed October 24, 2026. She agreed to be named (Jazz). Store name and town are kept private. Her answers are paraphrased closely; direct quotes are marked.


- **Floor moment (Q1–Q2):** the salesperson steps away and makes the customer wait while calling the owner. Example: "we got this item for $50 but I wanna sell it for $75" vs the owner wanting "at least $150 or $100". Lots of back-and-forth; when they don't agree the salesperson tells the customer "my boss said no to this bargain" and the sale is lost. "It's a little annoying being the bad cop." Last month her parents were away for the weekend and kept getting these calls.
- **Staff visibility (Q3):** yes, staff should see cost and margin, because they only think about their commission.
- **Enforcement (Q4):** hard restriction: "you can't sell this at a loss or you have to sell it with at least a 25% margin."
- **Cost basis (Q12):** freight, delivery to the store, vendor membership fees, floor space (opportunity cost), cleaning, polishing, repairs when customers damage floor items, and the labor to assemble pieces that arrive unbuilt. Rule of thumb: on a $100 couch, about 10% covers delivery, setup and upkeep.
- **Age (Q10):** ideally sell within 3 months; drop at 6–9 months; items two years old exist and should go. About 10% of the floor is older than 9 months and she wants that at zero. Selling aged items at the cost she paid is acceptable.
- **Vendor minimums (Q9):** most vendors give a minimum selling price, usually a little above cost, plus a suggested price, so galleries in the same area don't undercut each other. Respecting it protects the vendor relationship.
- **Permission (Q18):** yes, she can be named as an interviewed owner in the submission.
- Not answered yet: a dollar figure for one bad deal (Q15), delivery cost per trip (Q7), what she would pay (Q18 price).

## The questions we asked
Purpose: confirm what solution and form factor she would actually use, and get concrete numbers we can cite. Her answers are the "owner interview" for the research score and the opening data point of the video. Ask permission to quote her.

## The floor moment (form factor)
1. Walk me through the last time a customer haggled while you weren't in the store. Minute by minute: who called whom, what did they look up, how long did it take?
2. Where is the salesperson physically when they negotiate: standing with the customer, or at a desk? Would they check a phone in front of the customer?
3. Should staff see the acquisition cost and margin, or only the floor and a yes/no?

## Layer 2: keeping staff above the floor
4. If a salesperson wants to go below your floor, what should happen: hard stop, a text to you to approve, or allow it and log it?
5. How many approval calls do you get in a typical weekend and weekday? How long does each take?
6. Which concessions get given away most: delivery, assembly, haul-away of old furniture, bundle discounts? Roughly what does a delivery cost you?
7. How do you handle a multi-item deal (couch + table + bookshelf) today?

## Layer 1: setting the price
8. When a new piece arrives, how do you set the tag price? What multiple, by what category or vendor? What makes you go higher or lower: brand, material, color, trend?
9. What tells you an item is "hot" or "dead"? How do you currently know how long a piece has been on the floor?
10. When do you decide to mark down an aged item, and by how much? Does your floor drop for it?
11. For a sale event, how do you pick per-item discounts, how many tags do you reprint, and how long does that take?
12. What does "cost" include for you: purchase price, inbound freight, delivery, holding time?

## Data
13. Where does inventory live right now (Excel, QuickBooks, inventory app, paper, Drive)? Which fields exist: cost, vendor, received date, quantity, photo?
14. Do items carry a tag with a SKU or barcode? Would printed QR tags be acceptable?

## Value and numbers (for the pitch)
15. Think of a deal in the last month where staff gave away too much. How much margin was lost?
16. What would you want to see every Monday morning from this tool? One number.
17. What does the current way cost you: hours retagging, interrupted calls, lost margin per deal? Any figures at all.
18. Would you use this on Monday if it existed? What would you pay per month? Can we name you as an interviewed owner and a reference user?

## Fancy add-ons (only if time)
19. Do you walk the floor to decide markdowns? Would a floor map showing age and price help, or is a sorted list enough?
20. Would a phone walkthrough that builds the inventory list be useful, or is a spreadsheet upload fine?
